**Description (the same text on both stores)** (2591 of 4000)

```text
Ayo & Nchò is the seed-and-board game Nigerian families have played for generations, on a carved wooden board in real 3D. Sow the seeds, count the pits, take the fours.

Two games, the rules you grew up with
• Nchò: relay sowing, captures of 4 and the last 8.
• Ayo: captures of 2 or 3, close kin to Oware and Awalé.
• Change the rules in Settings: capture on 4 or on 2 and 3, relay or a single lap, the last 8 on or off.

A board you can almost touch
Carved mahogany, iroko or ebony under a warm lamp. The hand lifts every seed, drops it in its pit, and it bounces and settles where it lands; every capture is scooped into your trough. The knock of seed on wood is made on your phone as each one lands.

Opponents with something to say
Adaeze, nine years old and already sure she is winning. Auntie Bisi, who counts every pit twice. Nnamdi, your quick cousin. Mama Ngozi, thirty years at the market. Each has a face that reacts and a word for every moment, and the game keeps your record against each of them and a rating that shows how far you have come.

Learn as you play
• A one-minute lesson on the board for your first game.
• Tap a pit once to see where every seed will land, what you would capture and what it leaves open; tap again to sow.
• After their move, see what they just did, and watch it again slowly.
• Mama Ngozi tells you when a better pit was there, and shows you.
• Press and hold a pit to count its seeds one by one.

Play together
• Two of you on one phone: the board turns to face whoever is to move.
• A friend online: open a room, send its four-letter code on WhatsApp, and let them in when they knock. Eight quick shouts go across the board, and you can ask to take a move back. Playing a friend needs a connection.

A puzzle every day
One position, the same for everyone, one move to capture as much as you can. Share your result, and keep a run of days going. Seven days played earns the river stones seed set.

No signal? No problem
Against the computer, two of you on one phone, the lesson and the daily puzzle all play with no connection. A lighter board keeps slower phones smooth.

Gold, bought once
One upgrade, bought once: Papa Ike, the Master, Mama Ngozi's old rival and the strongest player in the game; and Mama Ngozi's review of your whole game, move by move on the board.

Looks for the board
The Benin bronze board and Grandpa's board, cowrie and palm kernel seeds. Looks only: they never change how the seeds fall or who wins.

No ads. No coins. No accounts. Nothing you can buy wins a game. Your games, records and settings stay on your phone.
```

**App Review Information → Notes** (965 of 4000)

```text
No sign-in or account. Against the computer, two players on one phone, the lesson and the daily puzzle play offline.

To try a game with a friend you need a second device or a browser: in the app tap With friends, then open the invite link (https://ayo-ncho.onrender.com/?room=CODE) or type the four-letter code on the second device, and tap Let in when it knocks. The game server may take up to a minute to answer the first time if nobody has played for a while.

In-app purchases (all non-consumable): Gold opens Papa Ike (Home → the Master pill) and Mama Ngozi's review (the line with her face on the Game over card after a game against the computer). The boards and seed sets are looks only (Home → the bag beside Settings, or Settings → Board). Restore purchases is at the bottom of the store screen.

The app sends nothing to us except the moves, the nickname typed and the fixed shouts of a game with a friend, held in the server's memory for that game only.
```
